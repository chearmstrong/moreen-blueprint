# Starting points for school projects

These sites are starting points, not evidence. This reference does not configure search tools, filtering or site restrictions. Cite a page only when its relevant content or result is available in the current conversation; do not claim to have opened a page when only a snippet is visible. If no publication or update date is visible, mark the date as unknown.

| Source | Useful for | Check before using |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Kids Web Japan](https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/explore/index.html), Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs | Japan's geography, schools, culture and everyday life | It is aimed at ages 10–14, so explain unfamiliar terms. Some statistics are dated. |
| [National Geographic Kids UK](https://www.natgeokids.com/uk/) | Child-readable country, nature and history introductions | Check the article date and verify changing figures elsewhere. |
| [BBC Bitesize](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/) | UK school-level explanations across subjects | Use a page that actually answers the child's question; a matching topic heading alone is not evidence. |
| [Natural History Museum Discover](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover.html) | Natural history, fossils and biodiversity | Keep observations, explanations and historical reconstructions distinct. |
| [Statistics Bureau of Japan](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/jinsui/) | A dated official population figure for Japan | This is written for adults; give the date and simplify the number. |

For checking whether online information is trustworthy, the [UK Safer Internet Centre's resources for ages 7–11](https://saferinternet.org.uk/blog/safer-internet-day-2021-education-pack-for-7-11year-olds-taking-a-closer-look) distinguish facts, beliefs and opinions. Use that as teaching guidance, not as a source of Japan facts.
