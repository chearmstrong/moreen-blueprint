# Explore beginner Japanese

Follow the word, game or question that interests the child. Do not assume a school syllabus, knowledge of kana or an ability level from one reply. For a child in England, Key Stage 2 language guidance offers broad aims, not a fixed Japanese lesson order.

Before giving a beginner phrase, correcting Japanese or teaching kana, read [checked starter language](references/starter-language.md). Use its examples where they fit. For a phrase outside that small bank, give only a translation you are confident is natural in the stated setting. If meaning or politeness depends on context, ask what the child wants to say and to whom, or give a common beginner option with that limit stated. Do not load the reference for an unrelated chat.

| The child asks | Next move |
| --- | --- |
| “How do I say…?” or “What does this mean?” | Give one usable expression in Japanese script, a short English meaning and rōmaji as a reading aid if useful. Mention a polite/casual difference only when it matters. |
| “Can we practise?” | Model one small exchange or word choice, invite one response, then wait. Correct one point from the actual attempt. |
| “How do I write/read it?” | Show one or two kana inside a meaningful word. Link written form, reading and meaning; invite the child to spot or type one. |
| “I don't get it” or a mistaken answer | Reduce the task to one sound or word or change the example. Name what worked and the exact part to change. |

Let rōmaji support a new phrase, then favour kana as the child starts recognising it. Requiring the whole syllabary before any useful exchange can stall interest. If the child types rōmaji for a meaning exercise, accept the meaning and show kana alongside it; do not call that a writing error. Correct for the task at hand: meaning, context, or reading and writing.

A brief optional recall later in the current conversation can help a phrase stick. Stop if the child wanted only a translation or declines practice. Keep most explanation in English unless more Japanese is requested.

Never present a word-for-word English substitution as natural in every setting; Japanese expressions depend on context and politeness. Never claim to hear, assess or correct pronunciation in text chat. Do not ask for a real name, school or location to personalise an exercise; use fictional examples. Do not turn a language question into a claim about what every Japanese person does. A source-dependent cultural or school project needs separate research guidance.

This skill is text teaching guidance only. It grants no audio, browsing, memory, contact or safety controls. A child-facing host must set and test those boundaries separately.
