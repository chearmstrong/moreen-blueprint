# Checked starter language

Use this small bank for beginner examples. It is not a fixed lesson order or a complete dictionary. Choose only what serves the child's question. The Japan Foundation's *Marugoto* Starter A1 [phrase index](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/assets/docs/download/starter_a/MarugotoStarterActivitiesPhraseIndex.pdf) gives the Japanese and its rōmaji; its [lesson outline](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/en/assets/docs/about/starter_activities_contents_en.pdf) places greetings in a practical exchange.

| Japanese | Reading aid | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| こんにちは | konnichiwa | A common daytime greeting; not the greeting for every time of day. |
| おはようございます | ohayō gozaimasu | Polite “good morning”. The Japan Foundation writes the long sound as `ohayoo` in its index. |
| ありがとう | arigatō | Familiar “thank you”; `ありがとうございます` (*arigatō gozaimasu*) is more polite. The index writes the long sound as `arigatoo`. |
| すみません | sumimasen | “Excuse me” or “sorry”, depending on the situation. |

Do not present rōmaji as an exact English pronunciation. In こんにちは the final は is written *ha* but read *wa*; the Japan Foundation's [romanisation rules](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/assets/docs/teacher/resource/starter_a/spelling_rules_of_the_Roman_alphabet.pdf) give that example. If the child wants to hear a word, say this text-only skill cannot play or assess speech and suggest a parent help with an approved audio resource; do not invent a sound assessment.

For example, if the child writes “Is the last letter of *konnichiwa* わ?”, explain that the greeting sounds *wa* at the end but is written こんにちは with は. Show the final character once and invite the child to find it; do not mark the guess as careless or launch into a full grammar lesson.

For first kana practice, use the Japan Foundation's [hiragana and katakana chart](https://lo.jpf.go.jp/japanese-language/for-learners/resources/) as the checking source. Work from a word the child already wants to know rather than making them memorise an entire chart before speaking. The Foundation's [Marugoto teaching guidance](https://marugoto.jpf.go.jp/en/teacher/qateaching/) explains that Starter A1 introduces characters alongside words and uses rōmaji as an aid. The [England Key Stage 2 languages programme](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/239042/PRIMARY_national_curriculum_-_Languages.pdf) supports familiar communication and links spelling, sound and meaning; it does not prescribe a Japanese Year 5 sequence.

The [Japan Foundation London learner resources](https://lo.jpf.go.jp/japanese-language/for-learners/resources/) include material for parents and younger children. A source listed here is a check for teaching guidance, not evidence that a live page has been fetched in the child's chat. Do not send the child to an external site or claim to have heard its audio.
