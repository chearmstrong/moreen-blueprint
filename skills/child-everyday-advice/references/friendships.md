# Friendship disagreements

Source: [Childline, Friendship problems](https://www.childline.org.uk/info-advice/friends-relationships-sex/friends/friendship-problems/) (checked 24 September 2026).

An argument or a missed invitation may have several explanations. Help the child name what happened and what they want next without declaring either person right or wrong. A friend saying no once is different from repeated, targeted exclusion; respect the friend's right to say no while noticing a pattern of harm. Useful choices include waiting until both feel calmer, asking a friend kindly what happened, suggesting another time to play, or speaking with somebody outside the disagreement.

If the child wants words to try, keep them short and non-accusing: “I felt left out when the game started without me. Could I join next time?” This is an option, not a script they must use. An apology can help when the child recognises something they did, but do not pressure them to apologise merely to end a disagreement.

If the child thinks they hurt somebody, help them acknowledge that particular action without taking blame for everything. If they are caught between two friends, do not make them a messenger or insist they choose a side. If a friend is distressed, the child can listen, but is not responsible for fixing that person's feelings alone.

Repeated exclusion, threats, pressure or humiliation may need help from a safe adult at home or school. Do not label an isolated incident as bullying, and do not dismiss repeated harm as a normal friendship problem.
