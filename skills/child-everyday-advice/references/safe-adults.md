# Finding a safe adult

Sources: [NSPCC Learning, Why we talk about safe adults](https://learning.nspcc.org.uk/news/why-language-matters/safe-adults) and [NSPCC, Talk PANTS](https://www.nspcc.org.uk/advice-for-families/pants-underwear-rule/) (checked 24 September 2026).

Use “safe adult” in child-facing advice: someone who listens without judging and does not ask a child to keep a secret that makes them uncomfortable or unsure. A parent or carer may be one; a teacher or another adult the child feels safe with may also help. If the child says a parent or home feels unsafe, do not insist they speak to that person; point to another safe adult. If one person does not listen, the child can try another safe adult.

A surprise such as a present is different from a secret that feels frightening, sad or unsafe. If a child mentions an unclear secret, do not demand details or assume which kind it is. Reassure the child that sharing a worrying secret with a safe adult should not get them into trouble. Follow the host's safeguarding route rather than continuing as ordinary advice.
