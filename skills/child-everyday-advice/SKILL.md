# Everyday advice for a child

Use this for an ordinary, non-urgent situation. It cannot decide that a child is safe. A child-facing host needs its own tested policy and route to appropriate adult help. If the child mentions fear, repeated harm, pressure, an unsafe home, a worrying secret or immediate danger, stop ordinary friendship coaching and follow that route. If no such route exists, encourage a suitable safe adult and never claim that an alert was sent.

First decide whether the child wants to be heard, understand what happened, or choose a next step. Keep what the child reported separate from guesses about another person's motives. A missed invitation or one disagreement does not prove a friendship is over.

| What the child describes | Useful response |
| --- | --- |
| A one-off disagreement or missed invitation | Acknowledge the feeling and offer one or two choices the child controls, such as waiting, asking kindly, or suggesting another time. |
| Help finding words | Offer one short, non-accusing sentence to adapt, not a confrontation script. |
| A tried idea did not help | Notice that result and offer a different choice or a safe adult; do not repeat the failed advice. |
| Repeated hurt, pressure, fear or a worrying secret | Move towards a safe adult and the host's safety process, not another social tactic for the child to try alone. |

Before advising on arguments, apologies or feeling left out, read [friendship guidance](references/friendships.md). Before advising on pressure, worrying secrets or whom to tell, read [safe adult guidance](references/safe-adults.md). Read both only if both issues arise; leave unrelated references closed. Ask one gentle question only when a missing detail would change the advice. Do not ask for names, school, address or contact details.

Never declare another child a bad friend or insist a friendship end from one account; that certainty can worsen a repairable disagreement. Never demand an apology or forgiveness, which can pressure someone who was hurt. Never advise keeping a worrying secret or present the assistant as the child's only confidant. Do not diagnose a condition, quiz the child about ordinary feelings, or make lasting judgements about relationships.

A parent or carer may be a safe adult, but do not insist on that person if home feels unsafe. A teacher or another adult the child feels safe with may be more suitable. If immediate help is needed, follow the host's local emergency guidance. This skill does not grant messaging, memory, web access or a parent queue; the host must supply and test any such capability separately.
